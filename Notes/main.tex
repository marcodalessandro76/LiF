\section{Pump-probe response from the non-linear susceptibilities}
\label{sec:theory}

This section collects the relations between the polarization and the equilibrium non-linear susceptibilities that
we use to compute the transient response of a pump-probe experiment, and the approximations behind them. The
notation of the codes and the details of the computations are given in the next sections.

\subsection{Polarization and non-linear susceptibilities}
\label{sec:theory_chi}

Fields and polarization are written as superpositions of complex exponentials~\cite{Boyd},
\begin{equation}
\bm{E}(t) = \int d\omega\,\bm{E}(\omega)\,e^{-i\omega t} , \qquad
\bm{P}(t) = \int d\omega\,\bm{P}(\omega)\,e^{-i\omega t} ,
\label{eq:fourier}
\end{equation}
so that $\bm{E}(\omega) = (2\pi)^{-1}\int dt\,\bm{E}(t)e^{i\omega t}$ and $\bm{E}(-\omega) = \bm{E}(\omega)^*$ for a
real field. Formulas are in atomic units ($\hbar = 1$); numbers are given in eV and fs ($\hbar = 0.6582$~eV\,fs).
The polarization is expanded in powers of the field, $\bm{P} = \bm{P}^{(1)}+\bm{P}^{(2)}+\bm{P}^{(3)}+\dots$, with
\begin{align}
P^{(1)}_i(\omega) &= \sum_j\chi^{(1)}_{ij}(\omega)\,E_j(\omega) , \label{eq:P1}\\
P^{(2)}_i(\omega) &= \sum_{jk}\int d\omega_1 d\omega_2\,\delta(\omega-\omega_1-\omega_2)\,
\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}(\omega;\omega_1,\omega_2)\,E_j(\omega_1)E_k(\omega_2) , \label{eq:P2}\\
P^{(3)}_i(\omega) &= \sum_{jkl}\int d\omega_1 d\omega_2 d\omega_3\,\delta(\omega-\omega_1-\omega_2-\omega_3)
\nonumber\\
&\quad\times\chi^{(3)}_{ijkl}(\omega;\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)\,E_j(\omega_1)E_k(\omega_2)E_l(\omega_3) .
\label{eq:P3}
\end{align}
The first frequency argument of $\chi^{(n)}$ is the output frequency, equal to the sum of the input ones. Since the
integration variables and the indices are dummy, $\chi^{(n)}$ can be taken symmetric under the simultaneous
permutation of the input frequencies and of the corresponding Cartesian indices (intrinsic permutation symmetry),
e.g.\ $\chi^{(2)}_{ijk}(\omega;\omega_1,\omega_2) = \chi^{(2)}_{ikj}(\omega;\omega_2,\omega_1)$.

\subsection{Pump and probe fields}
\label{sec:theory_fields}

In a pump-probe setup the field is the sum of a weak probe and of a pump,
$\bm{E}(t) = \bm{E}_{\mathrm{p}}(t)+\bm{E}_{\mathrm{P}}(t)$. We take an impulsive (delta) probe at the time $\tp$,
\begin{equation}
\bm{E}_{\mathrm{p}}(t) = E_{0\mathrm{p}}\,\bm{e}_{\mathrm{p}}\,\delta(t-\tp) , \qquad
\bm{E}_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega) = \frac{E_{0\mathrm{p}}}{2\pi}\,\bm{e}_{\mathrm{p}}\,e^{i\omega\tp} ,
\label{eq:delta_probe}
\end{equation}
so that all the probe frequencies have the same amplitude and a phase fixed by $\tp$. In our simulations probe and
pump are along the same direction, $\bm{e}_{\mathrm{p}} = \bm{e}_{\mathrm{P}} = \hat{\bm{x}}$, and we need only $P_x$.
From now on we drop the vector notation and the indices: $E_{\mathrm{p}}$, $\EP$, $P$ are the $x$ components and
$\chi^{(n)}$ the $xx\dots x$ components.

Inserting the sum of the two fields in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:P1}-\eqref{eq:P3}, the polarization is the one with the pump
only, $P_{\mathrm{P}}$ (all orders in the pump), plus the terms that contain the probe. We keep the terms linear in
the probe and up to quadratic in the pump. In $\chi^{(n)}$ the probe can take any of the $n$ input places, which give
the same contribution by permutation symmetry: this gives the factors 2 and 3 below. Calling $\omega-\Omega$ the
frequency of the probe and $\Omega$ the total frequency given by the pump,
\begin{align}
P(\omega) &= P_{\mathrm{P}}(\omega) + \chi^{(1)}(\omega)\,E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega)
+ 2\int d\Omega\,\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\Omega)\,E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega-\Omega)\,\EP(\Omega) \nonumber\\
&+ 3\int d\Omega\int d\omega_1\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\omega_1,\Omega-\omega_1)\,
E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega-\Omega)\,\EP(\omega_1)\,\EP(\Omega-\omega_1) \nonumber\\
&+ O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^2,\,E_{\mathrm{p}}\EP^3) .
\label{eq:P_pp}
\end{align}
The terms of order $E_{\mathrm{p}}\EP^n$ describe the probe photon at $\omega-\Omega$ that leaves at $\omega$ after
the exchange of $n$ pump photons; for the delta probe~\eqref{eq:delta_probe}
$E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega-\Omega) = E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega)\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}$.

\subsection{Non-equilibrium response}
\label{sec:theory_neq}

The transient response is defined as the polarization induced by the probe in presence of the pump, divided by the
probe field,
\begin{equation}
\chineq[\EP](\omega,\tp) = \frac{\PPp(\omega)-\PPo(\omega)}{E_{\mathrm{p}}(\omega)} ,
\label{eq:chineq}
\end{equation}
where $\PPp$ is the polarization with both the fields and $\PPo$ the one with the pump only. The difference removes
$P_{\mathrm{P}}$ and, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:P_pp}, $\chineq = \chi^{(1)}+\dchi$ with the pump induced change
$\dchi = \dchi^{(2)}+\dchi^{(3)}+\dots$,
\begin{align}
\dchi^{(2)}(\omega,\tp) &= 2\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\Omega)\,\EP(\Omega) ,
\label{eq:dchi2}\\
\dchi^{(3)}(\omega,\tp) &= 3\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\int d\omega_1\,
\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\omega_1,\Omega-\omega_1)\,\EP(\omega_1)\EP(\Omega-\omega_1) ,
\label{eq:dchi3}
\end{align}
and in general, for the term with $n$ pump fields,
\begin{align}
\dchi^{(n+1)}(\omega,\tp) &= (n+1)\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\int d\omega_1\dots d\omega_n\,
\delta\Big(\Omega-\sum_{i=1}^n\omega_i\Big) \nonumber\\
&\quad\times\chi^{(n+1)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\omega_1,\dots,\omega_n)\prod_{i=1}^n\EP(\omega_i) .
\label{eq:dchin}
\end{align}
No assumption is made on the pump. The frequency $\Omega$ given by the pump appears as a phase $e^{-i\Omega\tp}$, which
is how $\dchi$ depends on the probe time. If $\chi^{(3)}$ did not depend on $\Omega$, the integrals in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi3} would give, by the convolution theorem,
\begin{equation}
\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega t}\int d\omega_1\,\EP(\omega_1)\EP(\Omega-\omega_1) = \EP(t)^2 ,
\label{eq:convolution}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the response to the pump field at the probe time (and similarly $\EP(\tp)$ in $\dchi^{(2)}$).

\subsection{Monochromatic pump}
\label{sec:theory_mono}

We consider a monochromatic pump of amplitude $\EOP$, frequency $\wP$ and phase fixed by the time $\tP$,
\begin{equation}
\EP(t) = \EOP\sin(\wP(t-\tP)) = \EP(\wP)\,e^{-i\wP t}+\EP(-\wP)\,e^{i\wP t} ,
\label{eq:sine_pump}
\end{equation}
with the complex amplitudes $\EP(\pm\wP) = \pm(i\EOP/2)\,e^{\pm i\wP\tP}$. The pump frequencies in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dchi2}-\eqref{eq:dchi3} take only the values $\pm\wP$, and the integrals become
sums over them. In the second order term the pump gives $\Omega = \pm\wP$,
\begin{equation}
\dchi^{(2)}(\omega,\tp) = \chi^{(2)}_+(\omega)\,\EP(\wP)\,e^{-i\wP\tp} + \chi^{(2)}_-(\omega)\,\EP(-\wP)\,e^{i\wP\tp} ,
\label{eq:dchi2_mono}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\chi^{(2)}_\pm(\omega) = 2\,\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega\mp\wP,\pm\wP) .
\label{eq:chi2_mono}
\end{equation}
In the third order term it gives $\Omega = 0$ (in two ways, $\omega_1 = \pm\wP$) and $\Omega = \pm2\wP$,
\begin{equation}
\dchi^{(3)}(\omega,\tp) = \chiz(\omega)\,|\EP(\wP)|^2 + \chip(\omega)\,\EP(\wP)^2\,e^{-2i\wP\tp}
+ \chim(\omega)\,\EP(-\wP)^2\,e^{2i\wP\tp} ,
\label{eq:dchi3_mono}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\chiz(\omega) = 6\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega,\wP,-\wP) , \qquad
\chi^{(3)}_{\pm}(\omega) = 3\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega\mp2\wP,\pm\wP,\pm\wP) .
\label{eq:chi3_mono}
\end{equation}
$\chiz$ is the change of the response at the probe frequency (one pump photon absorbed and one emitted: Stark shift,
bleaching, \dots). In $\chi^{(2)}_\pm$ and $\chi^{(3)}_\pm$ the probe component at $\omega\mp\wP$ or $\omega\mp2\wP$ is
converted to $\omega$ by one or two pump photons. These terms contribute to $\dchi$ at $\omega$ because the delta probe
contains all the frequencies with a fixed relative phase: they interfere with the probe at $\omega$ and oscillate with
$\tp$.

Inserting the amplitudes~\eqref{eq:sine_pump}, $|\EP(\wP)|^2 = \EOP^2/4$, and the phases combine into the delay
$\tau = \tp-\tP$:
\begin{align}
\dchi(\omega,\tau) &= \frac{i\EOP}{2}\left[\chi^{(2)}_+(\omega)\,e^{-i\wP\tau} - \chi^{(2)}_-(\omega)\,e^{i\wP\tau}\right]
\nonumber\\
&+ \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\left[\chiz(\omega) - \chip(\omega)\,e^{-2i\wP\tau} - \chim(\omega)\,e^{2i\wP\tau}\right]
+ O(\EOP^3) .
\label{eq:dchi_mono}
\end{align}
Only the delay enters, i.e.\ the phase of the pump at the probe time. The term linear in the pump oscillates with
period $2\pi/\wP$ (2.67~fs at $\wP = 1.55$~eV) and changes sign with the pump, $\EOP\to-\EOP$; it vanishes in systems
with inversion symmetry, where all the even order susceptibilities are zero. In the quadratic term the $\chiz$ part does
not depend on $\tau$ and the $\chi^{(3)}_\pm$ parts oscillate with period $\pi/\wP$ (1.33~fs); the minus signs come
from $(\pm i)^2$, i.e.\ from the sine shape of the pump (a cosine pump gives $+$).

\subsection{Pulsed pump}
\label{sec:theory_pulse}

We now consider a pump pulse with a Gaussian envelope,
\begin{equation}
\EP(t) = \EOP\,\fenv(t-\tP)\sin(\wP(t-\tP)) = a(t)\,e^{-i\wP t}+a^*(t)\,e^{i\wP t} , \qquad
\fenv(t) = e^{-t^2/2\sigma^2} ,
\label{eq:pump_pulse}
\end{equation}
with the slowly varying amplitude $a(t) = (i\EOP/2)\,\fenv(t-\tP)\,e^{i\wP\tP}$ ($\sigma = 3.2$~fs in the RT runs). The spectrum of the pulse has two peaks, $\EP(\omega) = E^+(\omega)+E^-(\omega)$, with
\begin{equation}
E^\pm(\omega) = \pm\frac{i\EOP}{2}\,\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{i\omega\tP}\,e^{-(\omega\mp\wP)^2\sigma^2/2} ,
\label{eq:pump_spectrum}
\end{equation}
centered at $\pm\wP$ with width $\hbar/\sigma\simeq0.2$~eV: $E^+$ and $E^-$ are the spectra of the two
terms $a(t)e^{-i\wP t}$ and $a^*(t)e^{i\wP t}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pump_pulse}.

\paragraph{Second order.} With $\EP = E^++E^-$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi2} is the sum of two terms,
\begin{align}
\dchi^{(2)}(\omega,\tp) &= 2\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\Omega)\,E^+(\Omega)
\nonumber\\
&+ 2\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\Omega)\,E^-(\Omega) ,
\label{eq:dchi2_pulse}
\end{align}
in which the Gaussians $E^\pm$ restrict the pump frequency $\Omega$ to a range of width $\hbar/\sigma$ around $+\wP$ or
$-\wP$. No approximation is made. For $\sigma\to\infty$ the normalized Gaussians become $\delta$-functions,
$(\sigma/\sqrt{2\pi})\,e^{-(\Omega\mp\wP)^2\sigma^2/2}\to\delta(\Omega\mp\wP)$, and
\begin{equation*}
E^\pm(\Omega)\;\xrightarrow{\;\sigma\to\infty\;}\;\pm\frac{i\EOP}{2}\,e^{\pm i\wP\tP}\,\delta(\Omega\mp\wP)
= \EP(\pm\wP)\,\delta(\Omega\mp\wP) ,
\end{equation*}
with the amplitudes $\EP(\pm\wP)$ of the monochromatic pump~\eqref{eq:sine_pump}: the integrals select
$\Omega = \pm\wP$ and Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi2_pulse} becomes Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi2_mono}, with
$2\chi^{(2)}(\omega;\omega\mp\wP,\pm\wP) = \chi^{(2)}_\pm(\omega)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi2_mono}. For a finite $\sigma$
these are the values of the susceptibilities at the centers of the two ranges.

\paragraph{Third order.} The two pump fields in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi3} give four products
$E^a(\omega_1)E^b(\Omega-\omega_1)$, $a,b = \pm$, each different from zero only for $\omega_1$ close to $a\wP$ and
$\Omega$ close to $(a+b)\wP$: $E^+E^-$ and $E^-E^+$ with $\Omega\simeq0$, $E^+E^+$ with $\Omega\simeq2\wP$ and $E^-E^-$
with $\Omega\simeq-2\wP$. At fixed $\Omega$ the integral over $\omega_1$ runs over the ways $\Omega$ is shared between
the two pump photons, with $\omega_1$ within $\hbar/\sigma$ of $\pm\wP$. In $\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\omega_1,
\Omega-\omega_1)$ a change of $\omega_1$ at fixed $\Omega$ changes only the two pump arguments, which stay close to
$\pm\wP$, far below the gap: there $\chi^{(3)}$ changes on a scale of eV, much larger than $\hbar/\sigma$. So $\chi^{(3)}$
can be taken out of the integral over $\omega_1$, at the center of the peak $\omega_1 = \pm\wP$ (the second pump
argument, $\Omega-\omega_1$, follows from the sum of the frequencies). A change of $\Omega$ also changes the probe
argument $\omega-\Omega$, which is resonant: this dependence is kept. The two cross terms give the same contribution
(they exchange the two pump photons) and
\begin{align}
\dchi^{(3)}(\omega,\tp) &= 6\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\wP,\Omega-\wP)\,S_0(\Omega)
\nonumber\\
&+ 3\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,\wP,\Omega-\wP)\,S_{+2}(\Omega) \nonumber\\
&+ 3\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega-\Omega,-\wP,\Omega+\wP)\,S_{-2}(\Omega) ,
\label{eq:dchi3_pulse}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
S_0(\Omega) = \int d\omega_1\,E^+(\omega_1)\,E^-(\Omega-\omega_1) , \qquad
S_{\pm2}(\Omega) = \int d\omega_1\,E^\pm(\omega_1)\,E^\pm(\Omega-\omega_1)
\label{eq:S3_def}
\end{equation}
are the spectra of the pairs of pump fields. With the Gaussians~\eqref{eq:pump_spectrum} the integrals over $\omega_1$ are Gaussian too,
\begin{equation}
S_0(\Omega) = \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,e^{i\Omega\tP}\,e^{-\Omega^2\sigma^2/4} , \qquad
S_{\pm2}(\Omega) = -\frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\frac{\sigma}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,e^{i\Omega\tP}\,e^{-(\Omega\mp2\wP)^2\sigma^2/4} :
\label{eq:S3}
\end{equation}
they restrict $\Omega$ to a range of width $\sqrt{2}\,\hbar/\sigma$ around 0 and $\pm2\wP$ (the minus sign of $S_{\pm2}$ is
$(\pm i)^2$). At the centers the susceptibilities are those of the monochromatic pump,
$6\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega,\wP,-\wP) = \chiz(\omega)$ and
$3\chi^{(3)}(\omega;\omega\mp2\wP,\pm\wP,\pm\wP) = \chi^{(3)}_\pm(\omega)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chi3_mono}.

\paragraph{$\chi$ independent of $\Omega$: envelope at the probe time.} If the susceptibilities do not change over the
ranges of $\Omega$ selected by the pump (widths $\hbar/\sigma$ and $\sqrt{2}\,\hbar/\sigma$), they can be taken out of the integrals at the centers of the
ranges, and the integrals left are Gaussian. With the delay $\tau = \tp-\tP$, at second order
\begin{equation}
\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,E^\pm(\Omega) = \pm\frac{i\EOP}{2}\,\fenv(\tau)\,e^{\mp i\wP\tau} ,
\end{equation}
which are the two terms of the pump field~\eqref{eq:pump_pulse} at the probe time, and at third order
\begin{equation}
\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,S_0(\Omega) = \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\fenv^2(\tau) , \qquad
\int d\Omega\,e^{-i\Omega\tp}\,S_{\pm2}(\Omega) = -\frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\fenv^2(\tau)\,e^{\mp2i\wP\tau} ,
\end{equation}
with $\fenv^2(\tau) = e^{-\tau^2/\sigma^2}$. Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dchi2_pulse} and~\eqref{eq:dchi3_pulse} become
\begin{align}
\dchi^{(2)}(\omega,\tau) &= \frac{i\EOP}{2}\,\fenv(\tau)\left[\chi^{(2)}_+(\omega)\,e^{-i\wP\tau}
- \chi^{(2)}_-(\omega)\,e^{i\wP\tau}\right] , \nonumber\\
\dchi^{(3)}(\omega,\tau) &= \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\fenv^2(\tau)\left[\chiz(\omega) - \chip(\omega)\,e^{-2i\wP\tau}
- \chim(\omega)\,e^{2i\wP\tau}\right] ,
\label{eq:dchi_probetime}
\end{align}
i.e.\ the monochromatic result~\eqref{eq:dchi_mono} with the field envelope $\fenv(\tau)$ and the intensity envelope
$\fenv^2(\tau)$ at the probe time: the pump acts only through its field at the probe time.

\paragraph{Validity of the approximation.} The assumption is that the susceptibilities do not change over the range of
$\Omega$ selected by the pump, of width $\sim\hbar/\sigma$. For the pump arguments this is the condition already used
for the integral over $\omega_1$. The probe argument $\omega-\Omega$ is instead resonant: near a transition of energy
$\varepsilon$ the susceptibilities change with $\Omega$ on the scale of the width $\eta$ of the transition, as
$D(\omega-\Omega)$ with $D(\omega) = 1/(\varepsilon-\omega-i\eta)$. The condition is therefore $\eta\gg\hbar/\sigma$
(sharper structures, as an absorption edge, make it more severe).

In time this condition has a simple meaning. The delta probe starts at $\tp$ a dynamics of the system: it creates a
polarization that oscillates at the transition energies and lasts a time $\hbar/\eta$ (a line of width $\eta$
corresponds to a polarization damped as $e^{-\eta(t-\tp)}$). The response at the frequency $\omega$ is built over all
this time, and the pump changes it as long as the polarization lives, not only at $\tp$. What enters is therefore the
pump intensity between $\tp$ and $\tp+\hbar/\eta$, and the approximation replaces it with its value at $\tp$: the pump
envelope must be constant during the dynamics started by the probe, $\hbar/\eta\ll\sigma$. This is the condition
$\eta\gg\hbar/\sigma$ seen in time.
The approximation is best at the pump maximum, where the envelope is flat, and fails first on its slopes: before the
maximum the intensity grows during the probe dynamics and Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi_probetime} underestimates the effect of
the pump, after the maximum it overestimates it.

\paragraph{$\chi$ dependent on $\Omega$: accumulated envelope.} The susceptibilities of a monochromatic pump give
$\chi^{(3)}$ only at the centers $\Omega = 0,\pm2\wP$: the dependence on $\Omega$ requires a model, which we build in
time for the third order term. At $\tp$ the probe creates coherences between valence and conduction states, which
oscillate at the transition energies and decay with the width $\eta$. The pump, far below the gap, creates no real
excitations: its effect is to shift the transition energies while it is on. We assume that the shift follows the
intensity envelope of the pump,
\begin{equation}
\delta\varepsilon(t) = c_\varepsilon\,\frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,\fenv^2(t-\tP) ,
\label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
with a constant $c_\varepsilon$ for each transition (dynamical Stark and Franz-Keldysh shifts), and treat each pair of
valence and conduction states as an independent two-level system.

For the delta probe a transition of energy $\varepsilon$ and dipole $d$ gives a coherence that oscillates and
decays: the polarization at the time $t$ after the probe is $P_\varepsilon(\tp+t) = E_{0\mathrm{p}}\,R_\varepsilon(t)$,
with the response function
\begin{equation}
R_\varepsilon(t) = i|d|^2e^{-i\varepsilon t-\eta t} \, , \quad t>0 \, .
\label{eq:R_eps}
\end{equation}
With the energy $\varepsilon+\delta\varepsilon(t)$ the
coherence accumulates the extra phase $-\int_{\tp}^{\tp+t}\delta\varepsilon\,dt'$ and, to first order in
$\delta\varepsilon$ (third order in the fields), with the delay $\tau = \tp-\tP$,
\begin{equation}
\delta R_\varepsilon(t) = -ic_\varepsilon\,\frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,R_\varepsilon(t)\int_0^t\fenv^2(\tau+t')\,dt' :
\label{eq:dR}
\end{equation}
the polarization at the time $t$ feels the pump at all the times between the probe and $t$, with the same weight.

The integral does not depend on the transition. In the sum over the transitions we then need only
$\sum_\varepsilon(-ic_\varepsilon)R_\varepsilon(t)$, which contains the unknown $c_\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$ and $d$.
It is fixed by requiring that the model reproduces the susceptibility $\chiz$ computed with a monochromatic pump: there
$\fenv = 1$, the integral is $t$ and the sum of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dR} must give $(\EOP^2/4)\,R_0(t)$, with the response
function of $\chiz$,
\begin{equation}
\chiz(\omega) = \int_0^\infty dt\,R_0(t)\,e^{i\omega t} , \qquad
R_0(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int d\omega\,\chiz(\omega)\,e^{-i\omega t}
\label{eq:R0}
\end{equation}
(the factor $1/2\pi$ is in the inverse transform because $\chi$ is the ratio of the transforms of $P$ and
$E_{\mathrm{p}}$). Hence $\sum_\varepsilon(-ic_\varepsilon)R_\varepsilon(t) = R_0(t)/t$ and, with the pulse,
\begin{equation}
\delta R(t) = \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\,W(\tau,t)\,R_0(t) , \qquad
W(\tau,t) = \frac{1}{t}\int_0^t\fenv^2(\tau+t')\,dt'
= \frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\sigma}{2t}\left[\mathrm{erf}\Big(\frac{\tau+t}{\sigma}\Big)
-\mathrm{erf}\Big(\frac{\tau}{\sigma}\Big)\right] ,
\label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
where $W$, the ratio between the intensity accumulated with the pulse and with a constant envelope, is the mean
intensity envelope between the probe and $t$. The single $c_\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon$ and $d$ are not needed: $\chiz$
is transformed to time, multiplied by $W$ and transformed back.

In the terms at $\pm2\wP$ the pump enters through the parts of $\EP(t)^2$ that oscillate at $\pm2\wP$,
$\propto\fenv^2(t-\tP)\,e^{\mp2i\wP(t-\tP)}$, with period $\pi/\wP$ comparable with $\hbar/\eta$: they are not a shift
of the transition energies and the model does not apply. We apply the same $W$ to their envelope and keep the phases
$e^{\mp2i\wP\tau}$ (an extension, not derived). The transient response is therefore
\begin{equation}
\dchi^{(3)}(\omega,\tau) = \frac{\EOP^2}{4}\int_0^\infty dt\,e^{i\omega t}\,W(\tau,t)
\left[R_0(t) - R_+(t)\,e^{-2i\wP\tau} - R_-(t)\,e^{2i\wP\tau}\right] ,
\label{eq:dchi_accumulated}
\end{equation}
where $R_\pm$ are the response functions of $\chi^{(3)}_\pm$, defined as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R0}. For $\sigma\to\infty$, $W\to1$ and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi_accumulated} gives the monochromatic result~\eqref{eq:dchi_mono}. When the probe coherence decays
before the envelope changes, $\hbar/\eta\ll\sigma$, only $t\ll\sigma$ contributes, $W\to\fenv^2(\tau)$, and it gives the
envelope at the probe time~\eqref{eq:dchi_probetime}.

\section{Susceptibilities from the real-time polarization}
\label{sec:extraction}

The susceptibilities of Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} are extracted from real-time simulations: the equations of motion of
the electrons are integrated in presence of monochromatic fields and the polarization $P(t)$ is analysed in terms of
its harmonics. Monochromatic fields single out the frequency arguments of the $\chi$; the delta probe of
Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} is then built from them. This section describes the method.

\subsection{Monochromatic fields and dephasing}
\label{sec:extraction_damping}

Each simulation uses a sine field switched on at the time $t_0$,
\begin{equation}
E(t) = E_0\sin(\omega(t-t_0))\,\theta(t-t_0) , \qquad E(\pm\omega) = \pm\frac{iE_0}{2}\,e^{\pm i\omega t_0} ,
\label{eq:sine_field}
\end{equation}
with the complex amplitudes of the monochromatic field, as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sine_pump}. The frequency is fixed in each
run, and a set of runs samples the frequency range of interest.

The field is monochromatic only long after $t_0$. The sudden switch-on excites the system at all its transition
energies, and besides the response at the harmonics of $\omega$ the polarization contains free oscillations at the
transition energies. In a closed system they never decay and they would hide the response to the field. For this
reason a damping $\eta$ is added to the dynamics: the coherences between occupied and empty states decay as
$e^{-\eta t}$, the free oscillations die out, and after a dephasing time $t_{\mathrm{deph}}$ of a few times $1/\eta$, $P(t)$ is a
stationary superposition of harmonics of the fields. The damping is also
the width of the resonances: each transition gives a Lorentzian line of width $\eta$, as $D(\omega)$ of
Sec.~\ref{sec:theory_pulse}, and structures narrower than $\eta$ are not resolved. The simulation must last the
dephasing time plus the time needed to analyse the stationary signal.

\subsection{One field}
\label{sec:extraction_single}

In the stationary regime $P(t)$ is periodic with the period of the field and contains its harmonics,
\begin{equation}
P(t) = P_0 + \sum_{n\geq1}\left[P(n\omega)\,e^{-in\omega t} + c.c.\right] .
\label{eq:P_harmonics}
\end{equation}
At the lowest order in the field the $n$-th harmonic comes from $n$ photons at $\omega$ and the constant term from one
photon at $\omega$ and one at $-\omega$ (optical rectification, with the two orderings of the frequencies),
\begin{equation}
P(n\omega) = \chi^{(n)}(n\omega;\omega,\dots,\omega)\,E(\omega)^n , \qquad
P_0 = 2\chi^{(2)}(0;\omega,-\omega)\,|E(\omega)|^2 ,
\label{eq:P_single}
\end{equation}
with corrections of relative order $E_0^2$. The components are obtained by a least squares fit of $P(t)$, in a
window after $t_{\mathrm{deph}}$, with
\begin{equation}
P(t) = B_0 + \sum_{n=1}^{N}A_n\sin(n\omega t+\phi_n) , \qquad P(n\omega) = \frac{iA_n}{2}\,e^{-i\phi_n} , \qquad
P_0 = B_0 ,
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
which is linear in the unknowns $B_0$, $A_n\cos\phi_n$ and $A_n\sin\phi_n$. Since $P(t)$ is periodic, one period of the
field is enough to separate the harmonics. All the harmonics with a sizeable amplitude must be in the fit: a missing
one is projected onto the others. The susceptibilities are the ratios between the components and the powers of the
field amplitude, e.g.\ $\chi^{(1)}(\omega) = P(\omega)/E(\omega)$.

The linear response can also be obtained at all the frequencies with a single run, with a delta field
$E(t) = E_0\,\delta(t-t_0)$: then $\chi^{(1)}(\omega) = P(\omega)/E(\omega)$ is the Fourier transform of $P(t)$, with the
same damping. It is an independent check of the first harmonic of the sine runs.

\subsection{Two fields: frequency mixing}
\label{sec:extraction_mixing}

The pump-probe susceptibilities are extracted from simulations with two sine fields: a probe at $\omega$, one run for
each frequency of a grid, and a pump at the fixed frequency $\wP$, with the amplitudes $E_{\mathrm{p}}(\pm\omega)$ and
$\EP(\pm\wP)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sine_field}. In the stationary regime the polarization contains all the combinations of
the two frequencies,
\begin{equation}
P(t) = P_0 + \sum_{n,m}\left[P(n\omega+m\wP)\,e^{-i(n\omega+m\wP)t} + c.c.\right] ,
\label{eq:P_mixing}
\end{equation}
where $n$ and $m$ are integers of any sign and each positive frequency $n\omega+m\wP$ is counted once. We define the
mixing susceptibilities
\begin{equation}
\chin{n}{m}(\omega) = \frac{P(n\omega+m\wP)}{E_{\mathrm{p}}(\pm\omega)^{|n|}\,\EP(\pm\wP)^{|m|}} ,
\label{eq:chi_nm}
\end{equation}
where the signs are those of $n$ and $m$: the ratio between a component of $P$ and the product of the field
amplitudes that produce it. With this definition $\chin{n}{m}$ includes the number of orderings of the frequencies (the
degeneracy factor), e.g.\ $\chin{1}{1}(\omega) = 2\chi^{(2)}(\omega+\wP;\omega,\wP)$.

The components are obtained, as for one field, by a least squares fit of $P(t)$ with
\begin{equation}
P(t) = B_0 + \sum_{n,m}A_{nm}\sin(\Omega_{nm}t+\phi_{nm}) , \qquad
P(\Omega_{nm}) = \frac{iA_{nm}}{2}\,e^{-i\phi_{nm}} , \qquad P_0 = B_0 ,
\label{eq:fit_mixing}
\end{equation}
on the frequencies $\Omega_{nm} = n\omega+m\wP$ of all the components with a sizeable amplitude: the ones linear in the
probe and also the harmonics of the pump $(0,m)$, which do not depend on the probe and are much larger. Two
combinations with the same frequency cannot be separated. Now $P(t)$ is not periodic, and two components at a distance
$\Delta$ in frequency are separated only over a window longer than $2\pi/\Delta$: the window, which starts after
$t_{\mathrm{deph}}$, must be several times $2\pi/\Delta_{\min}$, where $\Delta_{\min}$ is the minimum distance between
the frequencies of the fit.

\subsection{Field amplitudes}
\label{sec:extraction_amplitudes}

The amplitudes of the fields must be small enough that the corrections of higher order to each component are
negligible, and large enough that the components are above the numerical noise of the fit (the weakest components, of the highest orders, are the most
delicate). The choice is checked by repeating the simulations with
different intensities: each component scales as $E_{0\mathrm{p}}^{|n|}E_{0\mathrm{P}}^{|m|}$ and the extracted $\chi$
must not change.

\section{Transient absorption of LiF}
\label{sec:lif}

\subsection{Real-time pump-probe simulations}
\label{sec:lif_rt}

The reference for the transient absorption of LiF are real-time simulations of the pump-probe experiment, one for each
probe time, which we performed with the setup of the simulations of D.~Sangalli. The electrons are independent
particles, with a scissor on the DFT band structure, and the dynamics includes the bands 3-6: the three highest
valence bands and the lowest conduction band. The k points are those of the kx16 grid (648 points in the irreducible
zone, reduced with the 8 symmetries that do not involve inversion or time reversal). The equations of motion are
integrated with the Crank-Nicolson scheme, without damping.

The pump is a Gaussian pulse at $\wP = 1.56$~eV with $\sigma = 3.22$~fs, i.e.\ the pulse of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pump_pulse}
up to its sign and a small term proportional to the derivative of the envelope, with the maximum at $\tP = 15.91$~fs
and the intensity $6\cdot10^7$~kW/cm$^2$, low enough for a third order response. The probe is a weak delta kick at
the time $\tp$. Fig.~\ref{fig:rt_setup} shows the pump and the five probe times, with delays $\tau$ from $-2.9$ to
$+3.1$~fs around the pump maximum.

For each probe time a run with pump and probe and one with the pump only give $\PPp(t)-\PPo(t)$, the polarization
induced by the probe in presence of the pump, Eq.~\eqref{eq:chineq}. Its Fourier transform from $\tp$, with a
Lorentzian smoothing that multiplies it by $e^{-\eta(t-\tp)}$ with $\eta = 0.6$~eV, gives the dielectric function
$\varepsilon(\omega,\tau) = 1+4\pi\chineq(\omega,\tau)$, and the transient spectrum is
\begin{equation}
\deps(\omega,\tau) = \varepsilon(\omega,\tau)-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}(\omega) = 4\pi\,\dchi(\omega,\tau) ,
\label{eq:deps}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}$ comes from a run with the probe only. Since the dynamics has no damping, the
smoothing plays the role of the width $\eta$ of Secs.~\ref{sec:theory} and~\ref{sec:extraction}: the probe signal is
collected over $\hbar/\eta\simeq1.1$~fs after the probe, shorter but not much shorter than $\sigma$.

\subsection{Even and odd parts in the pump field}
\label{sec:lif_even_odd}

LiF is centrosymmetric: the second order susceptibility vanishes, and the transient response is even in the pump
field and starts at third order, the quadratic term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi_mono}. The simulations, however, give also a
spurious response linear in the pump field, the term with $\chi^{(2)}_\pm$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dchi_mono}. The k grid by
itself does not break the inversion symmetry: it contains $-\bm{k}$ for each $\bm{k}$, with the same energies and
dipoles. According to our tests the spurious term comes from the coupling with the field, which is built from the
polarization written as a Berry phase: its derivatives with respect to $\bm{k}$ are evaluated on the discrete grid, and
this discretization breaks the symmetry. Indeed the spurious term decreases quickly with the density of the grid (by a
factor $\sim3$ from the kx12 to the kx16 grid in the $\chi$ extracted with two fields). The two contributions are
separated by repeating the simulations with the pump reversed, $\EP\to-\EP$: the odd part
$[\deps(\EP)-\deps(-\EP)]/2$ contains the terms linear in $\EP$ (second order), the even part
$[\deps(\EP)+\deps(-\EP)]/2$ the terms quadratic in $\EP$ (third order), up to higher orders, negligible at this
intensity.

Fig.~\ref{fig:rt_even_odd} shows that at $6\cdot10^7$~kW/cm$^2$ the spurious odd part is as large as the even one,
with a different shape; the same holds at all the probe times (ratio of the maxima of the two parts between 1.0 and 1.7
in 10-25~eV). The comparison with the transient response obtained from the $\chi$, which contains only the physical
third order terms, must therefore be made with the even part of the RT results.

The relative weight of the spurious term decreases with the pump intensity, since the odd part scales as $\EOP$ and the
even one as $\EOP^2$: at $6\cdot10^9$~kW/cm$^2$, the intensity of the simulations of D.~Sangalli ($\EOP$ ten times
larger), the odd part is only 6-13\% of the even one. At that intensity, however, the response is no longer
perturbative, and the RT results cannot be compared with the third order response obtained from the $\chi$: this is
shown below, with the results obtained from the $\chi$.
